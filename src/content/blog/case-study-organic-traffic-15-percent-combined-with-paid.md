<p>Most companies run SEO and paid media as two separate budgets with two separate agendas. When you own both, you stop treating them as rivals and start treating them as one growth engine: paid buys the test data, organic spends it on durable rankings. That's the setup at Autozilla Software Solutions, where I've been a Digital Marketing Specialist since May 2023. This is the organic half of the story — the same checklist I publish, applied to a real business.</p><h2>Start with the technical layer</h2><p>Before any content, the site had to be crawlable and structured. The audit covered exactly the items this portfolio itself demonstrates: one semantic h1 per page, no skipped heading levels, JSON-LD person and FAQ schema, canonical URLs, a live sitemap and robots.txt, and Open Graph tags. Each fix is friction removed between a crawler and the words we want ranked.</p><h2>Then a content audit — not more volume</h2><p>More blog posts don't fix a toxic keyword profile. The audit was about subtraction as much as creation: find thin pages, consolidate the cannibalized ones, refresh the pages that had ranking data behind them but stale copy, and only then publish new content where search demand existed and competitors were weak.</p><h2>The numbers</h2><p>Within the programme at Autozilla, organic traffic grew by roughly 15%. No single tactic did it — it was the compounding effect of clean technical foundations plus content that answers real queries, measured month over month in the analytics. The same period also ran 70+ qualified leads a month and Rs.2L+ in monthly ad spend, because organic and paid were pointing at the same funnel.</p><blockquote>Paid buys the test, organic buys the win. They only work when they're one game plan.</blockquote><h2>The part that does not publish</h2><p>Anyone can claim +15%. What makes the number believable is that it drops in alongside a live account with leads and budget — and that the same techniques are demonstrated in public on my own portfolio, crawlable by any engineer who opens the source or checks /sitemap.xml.
