<p>Most pricing advice is written for teams: product does X, sales negotiate, finance debates the discount ladder, marketing runs the value metric. When you build the product yourself, there is no debate — there is just you, the maths, and the customers who keep paying or leave. Hook AI taught me that. It's a marketing copy engine I designed, built, shipped and charged for solo, and the pricing felt harder than the code. Here is how I think about it.</p><h2>Price the outcome, not the hours</h2><p>The temptation is to price by effort: "I built this in X weeks, so it should cost Y." That's backwards. Buyers never see your hours — they see what the tool replaces. Hook AI writes conversion-ready hooks, headlines and ad copy across 15+ niches, and it bundles its own SEO program, email flows and A/B hero testing. That replaces work a marketer would otherwise bill. Price what it saves, not what it took to write.</p><h2>Let the middle plan do the selling</h2><p>Three tiers is a cliché for a reason. A cheap one anchors low, an expensive one makes the middle feel reasonable, and the middle is where most people land. When you build solo, the temptation is to drain everything into the cheapest plan to grow users fast. Users feel great, revenue doesn't. Keep the mid tier the one you actually want to sell.</p><h2>Ship a demo that feels insultingly generous</h2><p>Validation beats arguments. Before paying, people need a taste. A free, generous demo — one they'd feel guilty not paying for — does more than a landing page of screenshots. Reveal the value, then price the free experience appropriately.</p><blockquote>You aren't selling code. You're selling saved hours and conversations you no longer lose.</blockquote><h2>Chase the metric a founder actually feels</h2><p>Vanity metrics flatter — traffic, sign-ups, DAU — feel good but don't pay rent. The number I actually watch is revenue-per-visitor and the conversion rate at which free becomes paid. If a big marketing push lifts visits but the ratio doesn't move, you're attracting tyre-kickers, not customers.</p><h2>Pricing is a live experiment</h2><p>Don't set it once. Ship a version, watch the ratio, then nudge. Perhaps the hardest part of building solo is being both the person who wants to please every user and the one who keeps the product alive. The answer to both is the same: charge enough that the free users feel pampered and the paid users feel it was cheap.</p><p>The full success story and the real numbers will land here as the product grows. In the meantime — same one rule I give every client: price the outcome, anchor honestly, and track one real number.</p>
