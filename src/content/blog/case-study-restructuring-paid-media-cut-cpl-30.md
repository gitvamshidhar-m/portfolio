<p>This is the kind of account every media buyer has met: a lot of ads running, a rising cost-per-lead, and a ROAS that stopped improving months ago. The client wanted cheaper leads and more revenue — not a lecture. Here's the work.</p><h2>The starting point</h2><p>Cost-per-lead sat around Rs.1,100 and ROAS was parked at 3.2x. The account had grown without a clean-up: over 40 ad groups, overlapping keywords competing against each other, no consistent bidding strategy, and creatives that had never been properly split-tested.</p><h2>What was actually wrong</h2><p>Three problems, none of them unique. First, too many tiny ad groups meant the budget was scattered and the bidding algorithm had no data to optimise. Second, without a target-based bid, the machine chased volume instead of qualified leads. Third, heroes were guesses — a headline with no A/B data is not a decision, it's a hope wearing a budget.</p><h2>The fix, in order</h2><ul><li><b>Consolidated 40+ ad groups</b> into tight, themed structures so budget aligned with intent</li><li><b>Moved to target-based bidding</b> so the algorithm had a single number to optimise for</li><li><b>Started A/B testing creatives</b> and shifted spend to the winner instead of splitting evenly</li></ul><h2>The result</h2><p>Cost-per-lead dropped from Rs.1,100 to Rs.770 — roughly a 30% reduction — and ROAS lifted from 3.2x to 5.5x on the same budget. Across the wider program the work also contributed around 15% organic traffic growth and over 70 qualified leads a month.</p><blockquote>More budget doesn't fix a broken account. Structure does.</blockquote><h2>The takeaway</h2><p>The lessons are boring and powerful: consolidate so the data is clear, bid toward the target you actually care about, and test creatives instead of guessing. It's the same logic I bring to SEO and CRO — remove friction, give the machine a target, and let the numbers decide.
