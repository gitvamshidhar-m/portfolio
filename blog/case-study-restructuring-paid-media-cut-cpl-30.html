Case Study: How a Paid Media Restructure Cut Cost-Per-Lead 30% and Lifted ROAS to 5.5x

This is the kind of account every media buyer has met: a lot of ads running, a rising cost-per-lead, and a ROAS that stopped improving months ago. The client wanted cheaper leads and more revenue — not a lecture. Here's the work.

The starting point

Cost-per-lead sat around Rs.1,100 and ROAS was parked at 3.2x. The account had grown without a clean-up: over 40 ad groups, overlapping keywords competing against each other, no consistent bidding strategy, and creatives that had never been properly split-tested.

What was actually wrong

Three problems, none of them unique. First, too many tiny ad groups meant the budget was scattered and the bidding algorithm had no data to optimise. Second, without a target-based bid, the machine chased volume instead of qualified leads. Third, heroes were guesses — a headline with no A/B data is not a decision, it's a hope wearing a budget.

The fix, in order

  • Consolidated 40+ ad groups into tight, themed structures so budget aligned with intent
  • Moved to target-based bidding so the algorithm had a single number to optimise for
  • Started A/B testing creatives and shifted spend to the winner instead of splitting evenly

The result

Cost-per-lead dropped from Rs.1,100 to Rs.770 — roughly a 30% reduction — and ROAS lifted from 3.2x to 5.5x on the same budget. Across the wider program the work also contributed around 15% organic traffic growth and over 70 qualified leads a month.

More budget doesn't fix a broken account. Structure does.

The takeaway

The lessons are boring and powerful: consolidate so the data is clear, bid toward the target you actually care about, and test creatives instead of guessing. It's the same logic I bring to SEO and CRO — remove friction, give the machine a target, and let the numbers decide. If you've got an account that's plateaued, let's talk.
